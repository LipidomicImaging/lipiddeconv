Eight purposively selected singleton identities from CAL_R1_K125 were fixed before diagnostic computation: two true identities changing to zero rho, two false identities gaining the original cutoff, two retained truths, and two false identities passing the MILD cutoff. Numerical findings:

- Two true identities, PA18:0_22:3 and PA18:0_22:5, change from CLEAN rho7.648e-8/2.737e-8 to MILD scores between0 and9.009e-19 across methods. That range crosses the original1.273e-20 cutoff but is far below the MILD1.754e-5 cutoff. This demonstrates sensitivity of the original near-zero decision boundary in these examples, not that the entire recall failure is numerical.
- Their original full NNLS MILD coefficients are exactly zero. The false identity PA20:1_22:5 likewise has zero full-fit coefficient yet a returned rho9.009e-19. Removing a zero-coefficient column leaves a feasible fit with unchanged mathematical prediction; tiny independently recomputed residual differences are not meaningful evidence that the column is necessary. Agreement of several finite-precision solvers on such a tiny positive value is not sufficient validation of necessity.
- Three false identities have substantial MILD scores: PA18:1_22:3,8.475e-6; PA20:2_20:3,1.754e-5; PS18:0_18:0,2.295e-5. Across the three methods their absolute score ranges are at most1.8e-18. Thus these selected high false scores cannot be dismissed as the near-zero rounding phenomenon. All16 domain/identity classifications agree across methods at the MILD cutoff, including its boundary example.
- Observed KKT dual violations reach about9.8e-13 in CLEAN and5.6e-16 in MILD; complementarity is about2e-17 or less. These diagnostics are not rigorous error bounds, particularly for thresholds near1e-20. Long-double accumulation checks only residual subtraction, not high-precision optimization.

The initial diagnostic preflight stopped first on exact equality of two BLAS-derived scalars, then on tiny BVLS negative coefficients. Both logs and executed adapter versions are preserved in preflight_history. The final BVLS adapter records the raw coefficient minimum and projects only negatives within1e-12 times max(1, coefficient infinity norm); the largest observed projection norm was1.869e-18. Original NNLS/rho is unmodified. Projection is disclosed diagnostic bookkeeping, not a silent claim that BVLS returned strictly feasible coefficients initially.

Mismatch-channel findings:

We decomposed deletion loss by channel and used the predeclared top10% channels by absolute CLEAN/MILD mean-spectrum difference. This known synthetic difference is diagnostic-only; it is not measurement noise, an empirical background source, or an input to a deployable score.

For the three substantial false-score examples,61.5–73.2% of positive channel loss increases fall in that top10%. However, the two retained true controls also have62.0–62.4% there. Moreover, the net refit-correction vector has negative cosine with the total mismatch vector in these examples. Therefore channel overlap alone does NOT establish a false-specific mismatch-absorption mechanism, and the simplistic claim that false candidates directly align with the total spectral perturbation is not supported by this diagnostic. Nonnegative refitting redistributes multiple identities, which complicates attribution. Cosines and positive-gain fractions for near-zero deletion effects are numerically unstable and should not be interpreted mechanistically.

Conclusion: both a numerical decision-boundary issue and a substantive fixed-library identity-support problem are present in these selected examples. The latter remains after independent numerical checks. A low threshold that distinguishes tiny residual differences does not by itself provide reliable evidence, and merely fixing subtraction precision cannot remove the substantial false scores. The association with library ambiguity remains developmental, not causal.
